Eighth Point Privacy Policy

Effective date: 2026-11-12

Eighth Point is an independent companion app for the Riftbound trading card game, made by one developer. It is unofficial: not made, endorsed or sponsored by Riot Games. This policy says what the app keeps, where it goes, and how to delete it.

The short version: your matches, collection and settings are yours. They sync to your own account so they follow you to a new phone. Nobody else can read them, they are never combined with other players' data or published, and they are never sold. The app has no ads and no tracking. It records which features you use, under your account, so we can improve it. Only the developer can see this. It never includes your matches, cards or opponents, and it is never sold or shared.

What the app keeps

Your account. You sign in with Apple or Google. Eighth Point never sees a password. From the sign-in, our backend keeps:

  • your email address (if you chose Apple's "Hide My Email", the private relay address Apple gives us);
  • your name, as Apple or Google provides it (Apple shares it only on your first sign-in), and, from Google, a link to your profile picture, which the app does not show;
  • an account ID, and which of Apple and Google you have signed in with.

What you enter in the app. Saved on your phone first and copied to your account:

  • Matches: date, the Legends and battlefields played, scores and game-by-game results, the order and time of each point, who went first, format, duration, and anything you add: an opponent's name, an event name, link, round, type and venue, and a note.
  • Collection: the cards you add, with printing, finish, condition, language and quantity, whether you added each by hand or by scanning it, and how many copies you have sent to CardNexus.
  • Profile: the display name you choose, your Riot ID (game name and #tag) if you add it, and your favorite Legends.
  • Event nights: for an event you play, its name, the store's name, start time, kind, format and number of rounds, and your own standing: rank, points, wins, losses, draws, byes and the number of players. Nothing about any other player.

An opponent's name is something you type about another player or pick from the event's public pairings. It stays in your account only: no other user, including that opponent, can see it, and it is never collected into anything shared.

Kept only on your phone, never sent to our servers: the round timer, your round-length settings, a recovery copy of a match in progress, the match you are setting up (its opponent and event), an open trade, your recent scans and the sets you scan from, the opponents you told the app to keep apart, your CardNexus key and any send still in progress, the cached price list, the names of stores near events you looked up (a saved event night keeps its store's name in your account), and, in test builds only, a log of how long each scan took, which leaves the phone only if you share it.

Camera. The scanner uses the camera to read the card in front of it. The pictures are read on your phone and never saved to your photos, uploaded or sent anywhere. The App Store version keeps none of them. Test builds keep a small black-and-white picture of each card they read, the last 1,000 at most, inside the app to check the scanner's work; they never leave the phone.

Location. The app has a "Find my event" button. When you tap it, the app reads your location (asking permission the first time), rounds it to about a kilometre, and sends only that to playriftbound.com to list today's Riftbound events near you. It is not stored, and nothing about your account goes with it.

What the app does not collect: contacts, photos, microphone, health or payment information, advertising identifiers or browsing history.

What the app records about use

To learn which parts of the app are used and where people get stuck, the app records short events under your account. An event is a name from a fixed list, sometimes the screen it happened on, the time, a session number that groups the events of one sitting with their order in it, the app's version and build, and a few numbers, yes-or-no answers or choices from a fixed list. It never holds your matches, cards, Legends, battlefields, scores, opponents, names, handles, Riot ID, stores, events, searches, anything you type, prices or keys. While a match is on the board the app records nothing but its start and its end, and sends nothing.

These are all the events there are:

  • session_start: the app opened, or came back after 30 minutes away, with your account in rough sizes (how many matches and cards, as a range, and whether a CardNexus key is saved).
  • view: a screen or sheet was left: which one, how long it was open, and whether you did anything there.
  • match_setup_started: you began setting up a match.
  • match_setup_abandoned: that setup was cleared with no match started.
  • match_started: a match began, and its format.
  • match_finished: a finished match was saved: its format, how many games, and its length as a range.
  • match_reset: a match was left mid-play.
  • log_match_saved: a match you logged afterwards was saved.
  • roll_used: Roll for it picked a player, and which roll.
  • night_card_shown: match history showed an event night.
  • share_tapped: you tapped a night's Share.
  • share_sheet_opened: the share sheet opened, and the picture's size.
  • scan_run_started: the card scanner opened.
  • scan_run_ended: the card scanner closed: how many copies the camera added, how many cards were entered by hand, and for how many seconds it was open.
  • cardnexus_key_saved: a CardNexus key was saved.
  • cardnexus_send: a send to CardNexus finished: how many lines, as a range, and whether it went, partly went, was refused, was offline, was paused or had nothing to send.
  • trade_line_added: a card went on a trade pile, by search or by scan.
  • stats_filter_changed: you changed Stats' format filter.
  • export_done: you exported your match history, and as CSV or JSON.
  • restore_done: you restored match history from a backup.
  • error_screen_shown: the app's crash screen appeared.
  • error_reported: the app kept going past a failure: the name of the place in the code where it happened, never the error's message.

Events are stored with Supabase (see below), readable only by the developer, and never shown to anyone else, sold, shared or published. They are kept for 90 days; after that only weekly totals with no account in them are kept.

Profile has a switch, "Share usage with the developer". It starts off in the European Union, the rest of the European Economic Area and the United Kingdom, and on elsewhere, read from your phone's region; your choice follows your account to a new phone. Turning it off stops recording and clears the events not yet sent. It does not delete what was already sent: write to the address below and we will, or delete your account, which always does.

Where it goes and why

  • Supabase stores your account and synced data on servers in the United States, so your data survives a lost phone and appears on each phone you sign in on. Every row belongs to one account, and the database itself refuses to show it to any other account. Supabase also records the IP address and device type of each sign-in, as part of keeping sessions secure. The app also downloads a daily list of card prices from the same backend, using your sign-in; the list is the same for everyone and the download sends nothing else about you.
  • Apple and Google handle sign-in. They tell us who you are; the app sends them nothing back, except when you delete an account that uses Apple (see below).
  • Card images load from a third-party image host (Piltover Archive), and the app may ask it whether an image that failed to load exists. Like any website, it sees your IP address and which card images your phone requests. It receives nothing about your account.
  • playriftbound.com (Riot's event site). If you paste an event link or tap Find my event, the app asks it for that event's pairings, or for today's events near the rounded location above, and checks the event for updates while you play it. The app names itself and its version to the site. It sends nothing about your account: your Riot ID is matched against the roster on your phone.
  • CardNexus (optional). If you paste a CardNexus API key in Profile, the cards you choose to send go from your phone straight to CardNexus, under their privacy policy. The key stays in your phone's Keychain; Eighth Point's servers never see the key; the app syncs how many copies of each card you have sent, with your collection, so another phone does not send them again. Signing out removes the key from the phone. Nothing is sent until you tap Send.
  • Expo (app updates). When the app opens, it asks Expo's update service whether a fix is waiting and downloads it if so. The request carries the app's version, a random ID made for this install (so Expo can count installs; it is not your account and not a device identifier) and, if the app failed to start the last time, a short description of that error. It sends nothing about your account or what you do in the app.
  • Discord (only when you post a night). At the end of a Nexus Night you can post it to #nexus-night-shares in the Eighth Point Discord: you tap Post, the app shows you the exact post, and nothing goes until you confirm. The post is the night's share picture and one line with your saved handle, your record and the event's name. Our backend sends it to Discord, where every member of the server can see it, under Discord's privacy policy, and notes that the night was posted and when, so a night posts only once and an account at most five times a day. You can take a post back for 10 minutes; after that, ask in the server and it will be removed.
  • A TestFlight invite (only if you ask for one). In the Eighth Point Discord you can ask to test the app early: you tap a button and type your name and the email the invite should go to. Those, with your Discord user ID and name, go to a private channel of the server that only the developer can read. They are used only to send your invite through Apple's TestFlight, which then holds the name and email under Apple's privacy policy, and they are removed from the Discord once you are added. They are never matched to your Eighth Point account. To take a request back, write to the address below.

Exporting. Profile can export your match history as a file. The app hands it to your phone's share sheet, and it goes only where you choose to send it.

That is the full list. Your data is used only to run the app for you: sign-in, sync, and showing you your own matches, statistics and collection. It is not sold, rented or shared for advertising, and it is never combined with other players' data. There is no AI feature that reads your data.

Signing out and signed-out play

Signing out leaves this phone's copy of your data on the phone. A new match or card saved while signed out stays on the phone and is uploaded only if you edit it after signing in; a signed-out change to something already in your account goes to that account when you sign back in. The app assumes one person per phone; use account deletion, or delete the app, to clear a phone you hand on.

Deleting your account

Profile → Delete account permanently deletes your account and everything synced to it: matches, event nights, collection, profile, favorites, the usage events recorded under it, the record of nights you posted to the Discord, and the link to your Apple or Google sign-in. The deletion is immediate, with no recovery period, and there are no backups to restore it from. If you signed in with Apple, the app first opens Apple's sign-in sheet once more, and our backend uses the one-time code from it to tell Apple to remove Eighth Point from your Apple ID; if you cancel that sheet, nothing is deleted, and you can try again without it. The app then clears your account's data from that phone, including the setup draft, the open trade, recent scans, the opponents you kept apart, the CardNexus key and, in test builds, the scan log and card pictures; the price list and the round-length settings stay until you delete the app. Other phones signed in to the account lose access when their sign-in next refreshes; delete the app there to remove their copy.

Our hosting provider keeps its short-lived server logs, such as the sign-in records with your IP address, for its own limited period. Cards you already sent to CardNexus stay in your CardNexus account, and files you exported are yours; delete them there. A night you posted to the Discord stays there unless you took it back; ask in the server to remove it.

Removing Eighth Point from your Apple or Google account settings stops that way of signing in but does not delete your data; use Delete account for that.

If you cannot use the in-app option, write to the address below and the account will be deleted.

Security

Sign-in tokens are stored in the phone's secure Keychain (Keystore on Android). Data travels over encrypted connections. Access rules in the database, not just the app, keep each account's rows private to that account.

Children

Eighth Point is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect their data. If you believe a child has an account, write to the address below and it will be deleted.
